## Core description
A whimsical theatrical 3D history narrator with an enormous red/pink speaking mouth, white teeth and tongue, two mismatched blue/green eyes, and a distinctive tall black top hat. One character appears in three role variants.

## Identity locks
1. The Distinguished Historian, Eccentric Time Traveller and History Showman are the same character.
2. Keep the oversized red/pink mouth, teeth and pink tongue readable. Never replace it with a small human mouth, muzzle, beak or static grin.
3. Keep one blue eye and one green eye. Preserve the left/right assignment in the approved image when it is available. The eyes can move independently, but keep their colors and positions.
4. Keep the tall black top hat and its silhouette in all three roles. Accessories may decorate it but cannot replace or hide it.
5. Preserve the head/face layout, body scale, limb proportions, materials and key colors. Costume changes do not authorize a redesign.
6. Preserve the approved polished whimsical 3D finish; do not shift to photorealism, flat 2D, anime or another style.
7. Jaw, lips, teeth, tongue and cheeks move as one connected mouth. Teeth must not multiply, melt or flicker; return to the approved rest shape between speech.
8. Keep both eyes and the mouth visible during dialogue. Reject covered faces, duplicate characters and identity drift.

## Confirm against the concept image
Record head/body proportions, eye sides and placement, resting mouth, tooth arrangement, tongue shape/color, material hue, hat proportions, visible outfit, lighting and render finish from the actual image. Do not guess exact colors or geometry from this text. Generated turnarounds are proposals until compared against the source. Store palette notes and accepted sheets in assets/character/README.md.

## Role variants
| Variant | Use | Costume direction | Performance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinguished Historian | Context, explanations, careful transitions, closing | Scholarly formalwear, restrained details | Measured, attentive, lightly wry |
| Eccentric Time Traveller | Discoveries, period changes, travel transitions | Explorer layers, practical neutral accessories | Curious, quick to react, controlled gestures |
| History Showman | Reveals, openings, visual jokes, recaps | Theatrical tailoring, bold accents | Larger pauses and gestures, clear speech |

The hat and core model stay. If period clothing is needed, research it and use it as a costume overlay; do not imply the narrator is a real historical person. Accessories must not cover the face.

## Personality and conduct
Curious, warm, well-read and theatrical. Speak to intelligent newcomers. Admit uncertainty and separate sourced fact from interpretation. Use dry humor only when it fits the subject. Never joke at victims' expense in stories of enslavement, colonial violence, genocide, persecution or other suffering. The time-traveller framing is not a source.

Name and catchphrase need creator approval. Do not hard-code a temporary name.

## Voice profile
Creative direction, not a selected voice: warm mid-to-low register, clear consonants, conversational pace, lightly theatrical phrasing and controlled emphasis. Reuse one saved authorized voice and an episode pronunciation list. Avoid forced accents, imitation of a named performer and constant shouting.

For TTS, make a short calibration passage and check pace, names, emotion and mouth sync before a full script. Clone only the creator's voice or a voice with documented permission, following the service's consent process. Never store samples or credentials here.

## Expression set
Create a sheet from the approved model: neutral listening; explaining; curious head tilt with independent eyes; surprise; skeptical glance; concerned; restrained amusement; showman reveal; silent rest. Change pose and expression, not geometry. Do not pair comic expressions with scenes of suffering.

## Lip-sync requirements
Use the final narration as timing master. If the tool supports viseme/phoneme animation, map its supported shapes against that audio. Do not call prompt-driven approximation exact sync.
Minimum manual shapes: rest/closed; M-B-P; F-V; wide vowel; rounded vowel; open vowel; tongue-forward TH/L; neutral transition. Check M/B/P closures, F/V teeth-to-lip contact, vowel jaw opening and pauses. Review at normal speed and frame by frame during the pilot. Fix drift, frozen mouth and tooth/tongue artifacts. Keep dialogue phrases short if a model drifts. Use cutaways for long narration; never show a talking face over unrelated audio.

## Reference package
Before episode scenes, create: unaltered concept image; neutral front/three-quarter/side/back model sheet; face expression sheet; mouth/viseme sheet; full-body pose sheet; three wardrobe sheets; palette/material notes. Keep camera, scale and light consistent. Version each derived sheet and record tool/model, prompt version, date and review status. Never overwrite the source.

## Acceptance
Reject if mouth, teeth, tongue, eye colors/positions, hat silhouette, proportions or 3D finish change. Reject duplicated character, obscured dialogue face, deforming mouth, or unplanned costume change. Passing these checks does not establish originality or legal clearance.
